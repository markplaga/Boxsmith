Boxsmith Help

Boxsmith designs finger-jointed boxes for laser cutters (and CNC routers), shows them in 3D, packs the parts onto your sheet, and exports cut-ready SVG and DXF files. Everything runs in your browser, so nothing is uploaded.

1. Quick start

  1. Measure your sheet with calipers and enter it as Thickness. Real “3 mm” ply is often 2.8–3.2 mm, and that difference matters.
  2. Set your kerf. If you don't know it, pick Kerf test first, cut it, and read the answer off the comb (see section 16).
  3. Pick a box style from the grid at the top of the left panel. It plays a short demo of how it opens.
  4. Enter the size. Choose Outside dims if the box must fit a shelf, Inside dims if something must fit inside it.
  5. Check the Cut sheets tab to see every part laid out on your bed.
  6. Download the SVG (or DXF) and cut. Cut one corner joint first on a new material.

2. The screen at a glance

  • Left panel: every setting, grouped into sections. On a phone it sits below the preview, so scroll down. Use the Simple / All options switch at the top to hide the advanced sections (Joints & fit, Living hinge, Dividers, Decorate, Paint finish, Production and Saved recipes). Your choice is remembered.
  • Tabs: 3D preview, Cut sheets, Arrange boxes, Assembly guide and Help.
  • Arrange boxes: Place and save layouts of intact boxes.
  • Top-right buttons: Open… loads a saved settings or layout file, Copy SVG copies the cut file to the clipboard, and Download saves SVG, DXF or settings files.
  • Dimension card (top right of the 3D view): outside and inside measurements of the finished box.
  • Warnings (yellow bars at the bottom): things that won't cut or assemble well, with a suggested fix.
  • Status bar: number of parts and sheets, how much of the bed is used, total cut length, laser time, cost per box and suggested price.

4. Size and units

  • mm / in switches every length field. Files are always written in millimetres, which every laser program reads correctly.
  • Outside dims means the numbers are the box's overall size. Inside dims means they're the usable space inside, and Boxsmith adds the wall thickness for you.
  • For the polygon box, Width is the distance across opposite flat sides. For the book box, Width runs from spine to fore-edge, Depth is the book's height, and Height is its thickness.

5. Material, kerf and fingers

Thickness
Your measured sheet thickness. The Preset menu fills common values.
Kerf (beam width)
The width of material the laser burns away. Every cut line is moved outward by half the kerf, so fingers come out slightly wider and slots slightly narrower, and joints press-fit. Typical CO₂ lasers run 0.1–0.2 mm. Quick check: cut a 20 mm square, measure it, and kerf = 20 − measured.
Finger width
The target width of each finger. Boxsmith picks an odd number of fingers per edge so both ends match, so actual widths vary slightly. Keep it at least 2.5× the thickness so fingers don't snap.
My materials
A list of saved materials (thickness, kerf, sheet price and 3D look). Choosing one fills those fields. To add your own, set thickness and kerf, type a name under Save these settings as a material, and press Save. ✕ deletes the selected material.

6. Corner construction

Every corner of a box is a small cube where three panels meet, and exactly one panel must own it. This setting decides which.

  • Front & back: the front and back own all eight corners, and the top, bottom and sides have none. The classic layout.
  • Pinwheel: the top and bottom have no corners. Each wall owns both corners of one vertical edge, rotating around the box.
  • Diagonal pinwheel: each wall owns one top corner and the diagonally opposite bottom corner, spiralling around the box. On a square box all four walls are identical, so you can't put one in the wrong place.

7. Joints and fit: press-fit, screws, CNC

Fit
Press-fit uses your kerf as-is. Extra snug tightens joints by 0.04 mm for slippery or springy materials. Glue gap loosens them by 0.06 mm so glue has room and parts don't need hammering.
Screw joints (T-slots)
Adds captive-nut joints for M2.5, M3 or M4 hardware. Wall panels get a screw hole in a finger, and the matching panel gets a T-shaped slot for the nut. Set Screw length to the screws you own. These need stock thicker than the screw: M3 wants about 4.5 mm or more. Boxsmith warns you when holes would break through the edge. The 3D view shows the screws and nuts. Untick Screws to hide them.
CNC bit ⌀ (dogbones)
For router cutting. A round bit can't cut a sharp inside corner, so Boxsmith adds a small round “dogbone” relief at every inside corner. Enter your bit diameter and set kerf to 0, because your CAM software handles tool compensation. Leave it at 0 for laser cutting.

8. Fit and lid options

This section shows only the settings your chosen style uses.

  • Clearance: the gap for anything that slides or lifts off, such as lids, drawers, nested boxes and dividers. 0.3 mm suits most wood.
  • Lid height (split and hinged), Lid overhang (lift-off), Locator ring width, Pin ⌀ (hinged, pencil, puzzle), Lid capture lip (sliding, puzzle), Rim width (pencil), Front height % and Index cards (card box), Boxes in the set (nested), Acrylic thickness and Base margin (display case).
  • Top (polygon and rounded boxes): lift-off lid, fixed top, or open. Plate lip is how far the plates extend past the walls.

9. Dividers, drawers and living hinges

Dividers
Set columns and rows for an egg-crate grid that slots together and sits loose in the box. Height % sets how tall they are compared with the inside height. In the exploded view they lift straight up out of the box, with the ↔ pieces sliding off the ↕ pieces along their slots.
Drawers
Number of drawers and pull style; for a finger slot, Finger slot height sets how tall the slot is. Each drawer is an open tray with a false front that overlaps the case and acts as a stop. Dividers apply inside each drawer.
Living hinge
Used by the rounded box and book box. Cut pitch is the spacing between cut lines and Bridge is the solid wood left between cuts. For 3 mm ply, start with a pitch of 1.5–2.5 mm and 3–5 mm bridges. Smaller pitch bends more but is more fragile. Always test-flex a strip, and cut hinges with air assist.

10. Decorating: text, accents, patterns, artwork

Text

  • Type in the Text on the lid / top box. Press Return for a new line.
  • Alignment: Left, Center or Right. Lines line up against each other and the block stays centred on the panel.
  • Text is…: Raster, filled engraving (solid letters), Vector, outline only (a fast scored outline), or Cut through.
  • Preview colors (3D only): a Fill color and an optional Outline color for the text and its line work, so you can see the box as it'll look painted or inlaid. Burn resets the fill to the normal burnt look. In Vector, outline mode, Show the letters filled in previews a filled version. Line thickness sets how heavy the outlines are drawn (0 = automatic). None of these change the cut files.
  • Paint finish (its own section in the left panel): choose Watercolor wash or Acrylic paint and pick a color from the swatches or the color box. Watercolor is a thin transparent wash, so the wood tone and grain show through, and Wash strength controls how much pigment there is. Acrylic is opaque, with the faces in the exact color and the edges slightly darker. The finish shows in the 3D preview and in Arrange boxes, where each box keeps its own color, so you can set the paint before you add a box. It does not change the cut files. It is saved with the rest of your settings.
  • Line work around the text: rectangle, rounded rectangle, double rectangle, oval, notched label, Art Deco steps, scalloped edge, banner ribbon, brackets, or rules above and below. The Space slider sets the gap between the text and the frame, and Line weight its thickness. How many times repeats the frame (up to six, nested one inside the next), and Space between the frames sets the distance between the rings. Fill between the frames (3D only) gives each band between two rings its own color, or None. Frames follow the text mode (filled or outline). With cut-through text they're engraved, so the lid stays in one piece.
  • Text is converted to outlines inside Boxsmith, so your laser software doesn't need the font and you don't need a “convert to path” step.
  • For cut-through text use the Stencil font. Other fonts lose the middles of letters like O, A and B.

Corner accents

  • Eleven etched corner designs: simple bracket, double line + diamond, Art Deco steps, sunburst fan, filigree scroll, leaf sprig, solid triangle, dot cascade, chevrons, star + rules, scalloped edge.
  • Accent on: Top only or Top + four sides. On sides they sit inside the finger joints. Drawer insides and inner nested boxes are left plain.
  • Accent size, Line weight, and Etch as (raster fill or vector single lines).

Cut-out patterns

  • Fifteen patterns: honeycomb, diamond lattice, Voronoi, dots, stars, leaves, slots, Moroccan quatrefoil, hearts, triangles, crosses, waves, brick slots, fish scales and snowflakes. Cut them through the walls, the lid, or everything.
  • Face designs (at the bottom of the same menu): sixteen full-face cut-out pictures — tree of life, mountain landscape, ocean waves, compass rose, mandala flower, butterfly, crescent moon and stars, koi fish, sunflower, deer in the forest, tropical leaves, celtic knot, queen bee, gears, mushrooms and a snowflake. One design is centered on each panel you choose and the Design size slider shrinks it. Inner islands are held by thin bridges so they stay attached to the wood.
  • Pattern size is the cell size. Web is the wood left between holes; keep it at least the material thickness for strength. Shuffle re-randomises Voronoi.
  • Patterns automatically steer around holes, text, accents and artwork.

Artwork

  • Tap ＋ Add artwork and choose a PNG, JPG or SVG. Each image gets its own card with Panel, As (engrave, score lines or cut through), Width, Rotate and Move →/↓ from the panel centre.
  • SVGs are read as vector shapes, which is best for cutting and scoring. Photos and PNGs engrave as images. For score or cut, Boxsmith traces their dark areas.
  • Only closed shapes can be cut through. Open lines are skipped, with a warning.

Lid corners

Rounded or Chamfered corners on flat lids and plates (lift-off lids, pencil lid and rim, book cover, display base), with an adjustable Corner size.

11. The 3D preview

  • View options ▾: opens the less-used 3D controls: Stereo, Spin, Cut edge color, 💡 Lamp and Reset view. The bar at the bottom of the preview keeps Explode, Show it work, Screws, Material and Maximize in view.
  • 💡 Lamp: puts an LED in the middle of the box and closes it inside a room. Light streams through every cut-out, pattern and finger slot and throws shadows on the walls, so you can see how a lamp will look before you cut it. Only the box turns; the room stays still. Set the Light brightness and Colour (a picker plus presets), change the Room size, slide the walls with X / Y / Z, or press Reset walls. Clear and coloured acrylic let the light through. Press 💡 again to leave lamp mode.
  • Rotate: drag. Zoom: pinch or mouse wheel. Reset: double-tap, or Reset view.
  • Explode pulls the parts apart so you can see how they fit.
  • ⤢ Maximize fills the whole window with the 3D view and hides everything else. Use the Exit maximize button at the upper right, press Esc, or double-click or double-tap the preview.
  • ▶ Show it work loops the box's motion: lids slide, lift or swing, drawers pull out, the puzzle pin drops. Tap again to stop. Each style also plays once when you pick it.
  • Screws shows or hides the screw-joint hardware.
  • Stereo: the View options ▾ menu in the 3D bar shows two pictures side by side for 3D without glasses. Cross-eyed: cross your eyes until the two boxes merge into a third in the middle. Parallel: relax your eyes and look through the screen until they merge (works best with the pictures small). The Depth slider sets how strong the effect is. Tap-to-select is off in stereo.
  • Spin turns the model slowly. The material menu changes the look: birch, walnut, MDF, clear or coloured acrylic, cardboard. Clear acrylic is handy for seeing inside.
  • Cut edge color changes the thin, laser-cut surfaces in 3D preview and Arrange boxes for Birch ply, Walnut and MDF. Pick a darker brown for a more pronounced burn, or use Reset next to the swatch to restore the preset. Boxsmith remembers a separate color for each of these materials in this browser.
  • Tap a panel to select it. It turns orange, its name and size appear at top left, and it's highlighted on the cut sheet too. Tap it again to deselect.
  • Engraving, accents and artwork show on the model where they'll appear on the finished box.

Arrange whole boxes

Add complete boxes to a shared 3D layout to place them beside one another, stack them, or fit them inside an opening.

  1. Choose a style and enter its dimensions in the sidebar. Open Arrange boxes and click Add followed by the style name. The layout keeps a copy of that design. Change the sidebar to design the next box, and use Duplicate to make another copy of a selected box beside it, or Duplicate in place to put the copy exactly on top of the original, ready to move or turn. Each box keeps the material look it had when you added it, so changing the preview material later only changes the box you are designing.
  2. Click a box in the view or choose it from the Box to arrange list on the Boxes tab. The Place tab holds its position: X and Y position its center, and Z sets the height of its lowest point. You can also drag a box across the floor with the mouse (on a touch screen, select it first, then drag). Edges snap to neighboring boxes when they come within a few millimeters; hold Alt to move freely. Choose Place in top view on the View tab and click the desired location to place the center by eye. The layout and sidebar use the same inches or millimeters setting.
  3. To butt boxes together exactly, select one, then on the Place tab choose another box under Snap to, pick a face (right, left, back, front or on top, or for a Frame, a spot inside its opening), enter a gap if you want one, and click Snap. The box is moved flush against that face and lined up with its center.
  4. To move a box by exactly its own size, use the Step buttons on the Place tab: Left and Right move it one width, Up and Down one height, In (away from you) and Out one depth. A turned box steps by its turned size. Duplicate in place and then a Step makes a neat row or stack of identical boxes.
  5. To color a box, select it and use the Color row on the Place tab. To color just one part, such as a lid, tap the selected box again on that part; tap the part once more to go back to the whole box. For a box with acrylic, the Acrylic button colors every see-through part at once, and acrylic stays see-through, just tinted. A part's own color wins over the Acrylic color, which wins over the whole-box color. No color removes the color from whatever is chosen. Colors are saved with the layout and are only for the preview; they never change the cut files.
  6. To play a saved animation, click ▶ Import animation and pick the file. An animation file holds an animate command, usually with its own layout built in, so the right boxes load with it. If you have a different layout open, Boxsmith asks before replacing it (save yours first to keep it). Big files can take a few seconds, so a loading message shows meanwhile. A bar at the bottom of the screen then has Play/Pause, ■ (back to the start), a time slider and ✕ (stop and close). Picking a layout file by mistake just tells you to use Open layout.
  7. A whole collection at once: pick a .zip with ▶ Import animation and Boxsmith builds a 📚 Library panel from it. Each animation is listed by name, with its length and box count, a ▶ Play button, and a 🎬 button when the zip has a matching video. Each design (layout file) in the zip gets an Open button. Boxsmith asks once before the library replaces the layout you had open; after that, every piece just plays. ✕ hides the panel and 📚 Library brings it back. A remote's Layout & view tab lists the same pieces with Play and Open buttons.
  8. Animations play full screen. When an animation starts, whether from the library, the playback bar, an imported file or a remote, everything else is hidden: the sidebar, the controls, the library and the playback bar. The 3D view fills the screen, using the browser's true fullscreen when the browser allows it. When the animation ends, the interface comes back exactly as it was. To stop sooner, tap or click anywhere, or press Esc or the space bar. Recordings (Record WebM/GIF) play without hiding the interface.
  9. Boxes that collide are flagged with a ⚠ in the list and a warning above the controls. Boxes inside a Frame's opening are not flagged, but a box that touches a wall is. The check is by bounding box, so tilted boxes can raise a warning when they only come close.
  10. To rotate, use the Rotate buttons on the Place tab (⟲ 90° and ⟳ 90° turn it in plan view, Tip X and Tip Y lay it on its side, Reset uprights it), or hold Shift and drag a box left or right to turn it in 15° steps (Alt for free turning). For exact angles use Turn X, Turn Y and Turn Z (degrees). Use Hide box to see inside an enclosing box. Select the hidden box from the list and click Show box to bring it back.
  11. Save layout and Open layout sit at the top right of the controls, beside the tabs. Save downloads a JSON copy, and Open makes a saved file active. Remove deletes the selected box. Clear all removes every box from the current layout. Click it a second time within a few seconds to confirm. The ▾ button beside the tabs folds the controls away so you can see more of the preview.

Click Maximize to show only the layout preview across the browser window. Drag to orbit, or zoom with the wheel or by pinching. Press Esc or double-click or double-tap the preview to bring the controls back. Boxes cannot be dragged while maximized, so exit first to move them.

Two-screen editing (Remote)

Show the model on one screen and edit it from another, such as a laptop or TV for the model and an iPad for the controls.

  1. On the screen that will show the model, open Arrange boxes, tap 📱 Remote, then Show a code here. A 6-digit code appears.
  2. On the other screen, open Boxsmith, go to Arrange boxes, tap 📱 Remote, then Use this screen as the remote, and enter the code. You can also open the link shown under the code.
  3. Tap a box on the model screen and its controls appear on the remote, in three tabs. Every change shows on the model screen at once.
    • Place & color: Duplicate, Duplicate in place, Hide, Remove, Position, Step, Turn, Rotate, Snap to and Color. Tap the box again on the model screen to pick one part, or choose a part (or All acrylic parts) from the Color list on the remote.
    • Box design: the full sidebar (style, size, material, joints, lid, text, decoration and every other setting) for the selected box. Changes rebuild that box where it stands, keeping its place, turn, material and colors. Add this design as a new box adds a copy beside the others. The remote's own saved design is put back when you leave remote mode.
    • Layout & view: Fit layout, Top view, Place in top view (then tap the spot on the model screen), Maximize, stereo viewing, units, Save layout (it downloads on the model screen), Open a layout file from the remote device, and Clear all boxes (tap twice).
  4. The layout lives on the model screen, so saving, opening and the cut files work there as usual. Stop remote ends the session, and the remote is told.

Two windows on the same computer connect directly and need no internet. Separate devices connect through a small relay on Hugging Face; the first connection after a quiet spell can take a few seconds while the relay wakes up. The code only works while the model screen is showing it, and nothing passes through the relay except the edits themselves.

Animations, pictures and recordings

A remote can also be a script or an AI helper. It can send a whole animation in one message, and the model screen plays it smoothly, timed by the real clock. The Layout & view tab on a remote has Play, Pause and Stop, plus Capture view, Contact sheet (12 frames in one picture, each labelled with its time), Record WebM and Record GIF, and Import an animation from this device sends a saved animation file to the model screen. Pictures and recordings appear on the remote with a Save link, and a recording also gets a Download button on the model screen. Captures never change what the model screen is showing. Stop puts every box and the camera back where they were before the animation.

For example, this makes box 0 hop 60 mm three times while turning a full circle:

{"t":"cmd","op":"animate","args":{"unit":"mm","fps":30,"keyframes":[
 {"t":0,"boxes":{"0":{"position":[0,0,0],"rotation":[0,0,0]}}},
 {"t":0.5,"boxes":{"0":{"position":[0,0,60],"rotation":[0,0,60]}}},
 {"t":1,"boxes":{"0":{"position":[0,0,0],"rotation":[0,0,120]}}},
 {"t":1.5,"boxes":{"0":{"position":[0,0,60],"rotation":[0,0,180]}}},
 {"t":2,"boxes":{"0":{"position":[0,0,0],"rotation":[0,0,240]}}},
 {"t":2.5,"boxes":{"0":{"position":[0,0,60],"rotation":[0,0,300]}}},
 {"t":3,"boxes":{"0":{"position":[0,0,0],"rotation":[0,0,360]}}}]}}

and this returns a 12-frame contact sheet of it:

{"t":"cmd","op":"capture","args":{"mode":"sheet","count":12}}

Positions are in the display unit, the same as set (the example says "unit":"mm" so it means the same thing whatever your unit setting is). Turns of up to 180° take the shortest path; bigger turns are kept as written, so 0 → 360 spins a full circle and 350 → 10 goes the long way round (write 350 → 370 to cross 0 the short way). Positions, turns and the camera ease between keyframes, and each camera keyframe can have its own ease (linear, easeIn, easeOut, easeInOut or smoothstep) for the stretch up to the next one. Camera angles are never wrapped: 350 → 10 swings back through 180, and 0 → 720 makes two orbits. Add "cameraInterp":"catmullRom" for a smooth spline through the camera keys: each key is still reached on time, but the speed no longer jumps at every key. "projection":"perspective" plays the animation through a camera lens, with depth and parallax, and camera keyframes can then animate fov (the lens angle in degrees). The Orthographic / Perspective menu next to the stereo menu in Layout & view switches the view by hand, and Lens sets its angle; at the centre of the view, perspective frames exactly what orthographic does. Colors, hiding and design changes switch at their keyframe. The limits are 2000 keyframes and 120 seconds. Every command, including batch (many commands in one message), play, pause, stop, seek, capture and export, is listed with its options in the API reference (API.md, “Remote control”).

Choose Stereo · cross-eyed or Stereo · parallel from the viewing menu to see two views of the whole arrangement side by side. Adjust Stereo depth to change the separation. You can select or place boxes in either view. Choose Normal view to return to a single image.

Try a 240 × 180 × 50 mm Frame (torus) with a 24 mm ring width. Add three 55 × 45 × 30 mm Closed boxes. Set their X coordinates to −60, 0, and 60 mm, with Y and Z at zero. Click Top view to inspect the fit, then drag to see the layout from the side. Or add one box and use Snap to with Inside, centered to drop it into the Frame's opening, then Inside, against left wall to push it to a side.

Make a mosaic from a picture

The Picture mosaic section in the left panel turns any picture into a layout of painted boxes, and picks the colours for you. It runs entirely in your browser; the picture is never uploaded.

  1. Design the box you want as the tile, for example a 0.5 in closed box. Every tile in the mosaic is a copy of that box.
  2. Click Choose a picture…. A live preview appears under the settings.
  3. Set Boxes across (the number of tiles in each row; the rows follow the picture's proportions) and Colours (how many paint colours to use). Boxsmith finds that many colours that best represent the picture, and shows them as swatches under the preview.
  4. Click Build the layout. The mosaic opens in Arrange boxes in top view.
Faces up
Sides, alternating tips every box onto a side so the finger pattern shows, and alternates the two side faces like a checkerboard. Side, all the same uses one side face. Top, as designed leaves the boxes upright.
Paint
Watercolor wash is translucent, so the wood grain shows through (use Wash strength to control it). Acrylic is solid colour. The paint is a 3D preview and never changes the cut files.
Dither
Scatters tiles of neighbouring colours to suggest in-between shades. It helps photographs and usually makes logos and cartoons look noisy.
Skip transparent
Leaves empty spaces where a PNG or SVG is transparent, so only the subject is built.
Relief
Raises lighter (or darker) colours in even steps, each half a tile high, so the picture has depth when you tilt the view.
Add a frame around it
Adds a Frame (torus) sized to hold the whole mosaic, with the ring width you choose. The frame is as tall as the tallest tile.

Tips: start with 20 to 40 boxes across and 4 to 8 colours. Pictures with a plain background and bold shapes work best. A mosaic can have up to 4000 boxes; very large ones take a few seconds to build. If you change the tile design, size or unit afterwards, the preview updates, but you need to click Build the layout again. Building replaces the current layout, so use Save layout first if you want to keep it.

12. Cut sheets and nesting

  • Set your bed or sheet size with the Bed / sheet preset or type it in. Part spacing is the gap between parts.
  • Parts are packed tightly, and small parts are placed inside the cut-outs of big ones (for example inside a locator ring) to save material. Extra sheets are added when needed.
  • Grain lock stops parts being rotated, so the wood grain runs the same way on every panel.
  • Tap a part on a sheet to find it in 3D. Each sheet has its own Download this sheet button.
  • The display case puts wood and acrylic on separate sheets, labelled with their material.

13. Exporting files

Download → SVG
One file per sheet (zipped when there's more than one). Best for LightBurn, Inkscape, Glowforge and most lasers. Engraved images travel only in the SVG.
Download → DXF
Zipped, in mm, with layers CUT_OUTER, CUT_INNER, SCORE and ENGRAVE. Best for CAD and CNC software.
Copy SVG
Copies the file to the clipboard so you can paste it straight into your laser software.
Layers
Simple: red = cut, blue = score, black = engrave. LightBurn ordered: 00 engrave (black), 01 score (blue), 02 inner cuts (red), 03 outer cuts (green). In LightBurn these run in that order, so holes are cut before the part drops free.
Stroke
Line thickness in the file. Use Hairline or 0.001 mm if your laser software only treats very thin lines as cuts (Epilog, for example).
Engrave part names
Adds each part's name in small engraved text, which helps with assembly.

14. Production: batches, kits, cost

Boxes to cut
Lays out that many complete boxes (up to 500) across your sheets. Parts are numbered #1, #2…
Hold tabs per part
Kit mode: leaves tiny uncut bridges (set by Tab width) so parts stay in the sheet until someone snaps them out. Good for paintable kits and shipping flat.
Assembly card
On the Assembly guide tab. Downloads a printable page with a picture of the box, the parts list and step-by-step instructions, ready to tuck into a kit.
Cost estimate
Enter Sheet price, Machine rate ($/hour), Cut speed (mm/s) and Markup %. Boxsmith estimates laser time, the share of material used, cost per box and a suggested price. Times are estimates; check them against your real job times and adjust the cut speed to match.

15. Saving your work

  • Your current box settings and active whole-box layout are remembered in this browser automatically.
  • Saved recipes: type a name and press Save. Pick one and press Load to bring it back, or ✕ to delete it. Recipes stay in this browser on this device.
  • Settings file: Download → Settings file (.json) saves a file you can keep, back up or send to someone. Open it with the Open… button at the top, or with Open a saved settings file in the Saved recipes section.
  • Layout file Save layout downloads all box designs, positions, rotations, visibility and display units together. Open the file with Open layout or the main Open… button.
  • Reset everything at the bottom of the left panel resets the box settings to defaults after asking you to confirm. Saved materials and recipes are kept. To empty the active layout, use Clear all in Arrange boxes (click it twice to confirm).

Use the JavaScript API

Everything the page does is available from code: cut sheets, SVG and DXF files, parts, costs and assembly cards with Boxsmith.design.compute(settings); whole-box layouts with Boxsmith.assemblies (including snap, overlaps, measure and render, which draws a layout to a PNG); Boxsmith.mosaic; saved materials and recipes in Boxsmith.library; and the preview with Boxsmith.browser.view. Use Boxsmith in the browser console on the Boxsmith page, or in Node.js with boxsmith-node.js (run npm install jsdom @napi-rs/canvas first, plus playwright-core and Chrome if you want pictures). Loading only boxsmith-api.js gives you settings and layout files, without geometry. See API.md for every method. The API uses millimeters for dimensions and positions, and degrees for rotations. The layout’s unit setting controls how measurements are displayed.

Boxsmith.browser.showBox(tray);

16. Dialing in fit with the kerf test

  1. Choose Kerf test. Enter your thickness and your best guess at kerf (0.15 mm is a good start).
  2. Cut the comb and the joint pair.
  3. Push an offcut of the same sheet into each comb slot. Each slot is labelled with a kerf value. The one that grips firmly without forcing is your kerf. Enter it.
  4. Press the joint pair together. Too tight: lower kerf by 0.02. Too loose: raise it by 0.02.
  5. Save the result under My materials. Kerf changes with material, thickness, power, speed and focus, so test each new sheet type.

17. Troubleshooting

Joints are too tight / won't go in
Lower the kerf by 0.02 mm, or choose Glue gap. Check the thickness you entered against the actual sheet; it varies across a sheet too.
Joints are loose
Raise the kerf by 0.02 mm, or choose Extra snug.
A part doesn't fit my bed
A warning names it. Make the box smaller, use a bigger sheet size, or turn off grain lock so the part can rotate.
Text looks like the wrong font
The special fonts load from the internet. Give them a moment, or check your connection. Text is still cut correctly in whatever font is showing.
Middles of letters fall out
That happens with cut-through text. Use the Stencil font, or switch to raster or vector engraving.
Screw holes break through the edge
The material is too thin for that screw. Use thicker stock or a smaller screw size.
Living hinge cracks
Increase the bridge, reduce the pitch, use a larger corner radius, bend slowly, or mist the hinge area lightly with water.
Download doesn't start
Some views ask you to confirm each download, so accept the prompt. If it's blocked, use Copy SVG and paste into your laser software or a text file saved as .svg.
Settings or recipes disappeared
They live in one browser on one device. Clearing browser data removes them. Keep a settings file (.json) as a backup.

18. Glossary

Kerf
The width of material the laser (or bit) removes.
Finger joint
Interlocking rectangular tabs and slots that join two panels at a right angle.
Corner ownership
Which panel fills the small cube where three panels meet.
T-slot
A T-shaped cut-out in a panel's edge that holds a nut, so a screw from the next panel can clamp the joint.
Dogbone
A small round relief at an inside corner so a round router bit can fully clear it.
Living hinge
A pattern of cuts that lets rigid sheet material bend.
Raster engraving
The laser sweeps back and forth to fill an area.
Vector scoring
The laser traces a line at low power without cutting through.
Hold tab
A tiny uncut bridge that keeps a part attached to the sheet.
Nesting
Packing parts onto a sheet to use as little material as possible.
